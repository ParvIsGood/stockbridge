# Stockbridge: Market Research Brief (India & Punjab)

**Date:** November 2026  
**Stage:** Prototype-stage student startup  
**Product Summary:** Kirana shops capture item-level sales by voice, cross-checked against incoming UPI payment amounts. A distributor's field agent uses those real-time sales signals plus batch and expiry data to reallocate near-expiry FMCG stock to nearby high-velocity shops along the existing delivery route. **Pricing Model:** Free for kirana shops; distributors pay per active shop per month.

> **Note on Methodology:** All figures are cited with source links and dates. Where official government or trade-body data is unavailable (e.g., Punjab-specific kirana counts or route-level unit economics), figures are explicitly tagged as **[Estimate]** with stated assumptions.

---

## 1. Market Universe: Kirana Stores, FMCG Distributors & Route Economics

### Kirana / General Stores (India & Punjab)
* **India Total:** Approximately **13 million (1.3 crore)** traditional kirana stores operate across India, according to FICCI and the All India Consumer Products Distributors' Federation (AICPDF) ([Chitrangana / FICCI, 2026](https://www.chitrangana.com/india-retail-transformation-2030/); [NDTV Profit / AICPDF, Jun 2026](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/business/fmcg-distributors-warn-of-nationwide-protest-seek-higher-margins-amid-rising-costs-11608178)).
* **Grocery Market Share:** Kiranas account for **~91%** of India's grocery market in 2025 (projected to remain **~85%** by 2030) per Redseer Strategy Consultants ([Chitrangana / Redseer, May 2026](https://www.chitrangana.com/india-retail-transformation-2030/)) and over **80%** of total Indian FMCG retail sales ([SpireStock, Oct 2025](https://spirestock.com/blog/kirana-store-distribution-technology)).
* **Formal Modernisation Gap:** Invest India estimates only **~1.4 million** of the 13 million kiranas are expected to complete formal digital transformation on their own ([Chitrangana / Invest India, 2026](https://www.chitrangana.com/india-retail-transformation-2030/)).
* **Punjab Kirana Count [Estimate]:** No official central census isolates Punjab's kirana count. Using Punjab's population share (~2.2% of India) adjusted upward slightly for higher per-capita income and urban/semi-urban retail density (~2.5%–3.0% of national retail outlets), Punjab has an estimated **325,000 to 390,000 kirana / general stores** **[Estimate]**, concentrated heavily in Ludhiana, Amritsar, Jalandhar, Patiala, and the Mohali–Kharar belt.

### FMCG Distributors & Stockists
* **India Total:** The All India Consumer Products Distributors' Federation (AICPDF) represents **over 4.5 lakh (450,000) FMCG distributors** across 25 states, servicing over 1.3 crore kirana outlets ([Outlook Business, Jun 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/fmcg-distributors-association-warns-of-protests-over-margins); [NDTV Profit, Jun 2026](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/business/fmcg-distributors-warn-of-nationwide-protest-seek-higher-margins-amid-rising-costs-11608178)).
* **Punjab Distributor Count [Estimate]:** Applying a proportional ~2.5%–3.0% share of the 4.5 lakh national base yields an estimated **11,000 to 13,500 FMCG distributors and stockists** in Punjab **[Estimate]**.

### Typical Route & Beat Economics
* **Outlets per Daily Beat (Route):** Field salesmen and delivery vans typically cover **20–25 outlet visits/stops per day** manually, rising to **30–40 stops/day** with route/beat optimization software ([SpireStock, Feb 2026](https://spirestock.com/blog/fmcg-distributor-margin-profit-guide-india)).
* **Routes per Distributor & Total Active Outlets [Estimate]:** A typical mid-size Indian FMCG distributor runs **4 to 6 weekly beats (routes)** (Monday–Saturday) per delivery van/salesman, covering **150 to 300 active kirana shops** per distributor on a weekly or fortnightly replenishment cycle **[Estimate]**. (Implied national ratio: 13M shops ÷ 450K distributors ≈ ~29 exclusive shops per distributor, though in practice each kirana buys from 5–10 different brand distributors, making 150–300 active outlets per distributor the operational norm).

## 2. Expiry & Damage Losses in Indian FMCG General Trade

### Magnitude of Losses
* **Industry Write-Off Rate:** Expiry and damage write-offs typically represent **0.5% to 3.0%** of a distributor's purchase value/turnover—and **2% to 5%** for perishable/dairy categories ([SpireStock, Feb 2026](https://spirestock.com/blog/fmcg-distributor-margin-profit-guide-india); [ShelfLifePro, 2026](https://shelflifepro.in/fmcg-expiry-tracking-india/)).
* **Rupee Impact on a Mid-Size Distributor:** On a ₹15 crore annual turnover, a mid-size FMCG distributor loses **₹22–45 lakh per year** to expired stock (1.5%–3% write-off) ([ShelfLifePro, 2026](https://shelflifepro.in/fmcg-expiry-tracking-india/)). On a smaller ₹20 lakh/month distributor, spoilage and expiry cost **₹10,000–40,000/month** (0.5%–2% of revenue), plus **₹5,000–15,000/month** in returns processing ([SpireStock, Feb 2026](https://spirestock.com/blog/fmcg-distributor-margin-profit-guide-india)).
* **Root Cause:** An estimated **30%–40%** of retail shrinkage stems directly from poor FIFO/FEFO (First-Expired, First-Out) stock rotation at the store and van level ([Channelplay, 2026](https://www.channelplay.in/insight/fefo-compliance-fmcg-retail)).

### Who Bears the Loss & How Claims Work
* **Contractual Gap:** Most contracts between FMCG companies and distributors **do not include mandatory clauses** for compensation of damaged or expired stock ([Business Standard, Jun 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/distributors-body-urges-fmcg-cos-to-collect-damaged-and-expired-goods-125061601108_1.html)).
* **Claims & Recovery Friction:** Theoretically, **30%–50%** of expired stock is recoverable as principal-company credit notes, but **~50% of expiry claims are rejected** due to missing batch/lot proof, leaving distributors recovering only ₹3–8 lakh out of ₹7–22 lakh theoretically owed on a ₹15 Cr turnover ([ShelfLifePro, 2026](https://shelflifepro.in/fmcg-expiry-tracking-india/)).
* **Retailer vs. Distributor Burden:** Retailers push expired stock back to distributors expecting credit; when the principal company delays or rejects the claim, the distributor absorbs the working capital hit.

### Trade-Body Statements (AICPDF)
* **June 2025 (Open Letter):** AICPDF urged FMCG companies to formally collect damaged/expired goods from the market and provide timely, transparent stock replacement or compensation, highlighting the lack of contractual protection ([Business Standard, Jun 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/distributors-body-urges-fmcg-cos-to-collect-damaged-and-expired-goods-125061601108_1.html)).
* **April 2026 (Government Intervention):** AICPDF sought a government crackdown on unattended expired products, warning of a **"public health nightmare"** after nearly **₹5 crore** of expired and damaged stock was reported lying uncollected at distributor facilities in Kerala ([NDTV Profit, Apr 2026](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/business/fmcg-distributor-body-seeks-govt-crackdown-on-expiry-products-warns-of-public-health-nightmare-11394773)).

## 3. Kirana Working Capital, Stock-Outs, Software & UPI Adoption

* **Working Capital & Dead Stock:** Kirana owners operate on tight working capital, with distributors extending **7–21 days of informal credit** in general trade ([SpireStock, Feb 2026](https://spirestock.com/blog/fmcg-distributor-margin-profit-guide-india)). Industry estimates suggest **10%–15%** of a kirana's working capital is routinely trapped in slow-moving or near-expiry SKUs **[Estimate]**. Notably, **~40%** of retailers who shifted to digital procurement via ONDC's DigiDukaan reported lower inventory holdings ([World Trade Scanner, Sep 2026](https://worldtradescanner.com/Kirana%20Grocery%20Shops%20Linked%20to%20Suppliers%20thru%20Digital%20Links.htm)).
* **Stock-Out Losses [Estimate]:** Because kiranas lack item-level inventory tracking, stock-outs on fast-moving SKUs typically cost traditional Indian retailers **5%–8% of potential monthly sales** **[Estimate]**.
* **Billing & Inventory Software Adoption:** Adoption remains low. Only **~32%** of kirana stores have adopted digital payment or basic inventory tools ([Scribd / Industry Survey, 2024](https://www.scribd.com/document/1012404749/Impact-of-Quick-Commerce-on-Traditional-Kirana-Stores-in-India)), and formal item-level POS/billing software penetration among traditional kiranas is estimated at **under 10%–15%** **[Estimate]** due to typing friction (a 5-item bill takes ~40 taps on standard billing apps) ([VoiceKhata, Nov 2026](https://voicekhata.com/)).
* **UPI & AI Adoption:**
  * National UPI volume reached **23,201.93 million transactions** worth **₹29.90 lakh crore** in May 2026 (NPCI) ([Chitrangana / NPCI, May 2026](https://www.chitrangana.com/india-retail-transformation-2030/)).
  * According to PayNearby's *MSME Digital Index Report 2026* (surveying 10,000 retailers/MSMEs including kiranas), **UPI and Aadhaar banking drive 61% of MSME transactions**, and **71% of retailers** have experimented with AI tools for daily operations ([ET Enterprise AI, Jun 2026](https://enterpriseai.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/digital-transformation-in-indian-msmes-upi-aadhaar-drive-61-transactions/131961386)).

## 4. Distributor Margins & Current Software Spend

### Distributor Margins
* **Gross Margins:** **3%–8%** for packaged FMCG goods (3%–6% for staples, 4%–8% for personal/home care, 5%–10% for snacks) and **8%–15%** for dairy ([SpireStock, Feb 2026](https://spirestock.com/blog/fmcg-distributor-margin-profit-guide-india)).
* **Net Margins:** After logistics, warehousing, credit defaults, and expiry write-offs, net margins compress to **1.5%–5%** (most distributors earn **2%–4%** net) ([SpireStock, Feb 2026](https://spirestock.com/blog/fmcg-distributor-margin-profit-guide-india)).
* **Margin Pressure:** In June 2026, AICPDF warned of nationwide protests, stating that current **3.5%–5%** margins are no longer sufficient to cover rising fuel, manpower, and financing costs ([Outlook Business, Jun 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/fmcg-distributors-association-warns-of-protests-over-margins)).

### Quick-Commerce Pressure on Kiranas
* **Rapid Growth:** Indian quick commerce grew from **$6.1B in CY2024** to an estimated **$12.9B (₹1.07 lakh crore) in CY2025E**, with 4,500+ dark stores ([Datum Intelligence, 2025](https://reports.datumintell.in/reports/Consumer-insights/quick-commerce/quick-commerce-india-2024.html)).
* **Kirana Displacement:** **67%** of surveyed kirana owners reported lower sales since quick commerce arrived, and **$1.28 billion** in annual kirana value migrated to quick commerce in 2024 ([Datum Intelligence, Oct 2024](https://datumintell.com/report-quick-commerce.html)). AICPDF reported roughly **200,000 kirana store closures in 2024** ([FICCI, Jul 2026](https://ficci.in/public/storage/SPDocument/24363/step-up-scalable-transformation-for-efficiency-and-profitability-to-unleash-progress.pdf)).
* **Kirana Resilience:** Despite urban pressure, kiranas retain **>85% of India's grocery market**, defended by informal credit (100% of ICC survey respondents), small-pack/sachet assortment (87%), and personal relationships ([Indian Chamber of Commerce, Oct 2025](https://indianchamber.org/upload/page/pdf/quick-commerce.pdf)).

## 6. Government & Ecosystem Hooks

* **ONDC & DigiDukaan:** As of **September 2026**, ONDC's **DigiDukaan** initiative has connected **over 21,000 kirana retailers** with **450+ brands** (including HUL, ITC, Marico, Britannia, Coca-Cola) across Hyderabad, Jaipur, and Mumbai, focusing on standardised catalogues, DMS connectors, and digital credit ([World Trade Scanner, Sep 2026](https://worldtradescanner.com/Kirana%20Grocery%20Shops%20Linked%20to%20Suppliers%20thru%20Digital%20Links.htm)). *Hook for Stockbridge:* Integrate with ONDC/DMS standards to position Stockbridge as a zero-friction inventory layer.
* **Digital India & MSME Schemes:** Udyam registration (which grants formal MSME status to retail/wholesale trade), CGTMSE collateral-free credit guarantees, and UPI/Account Aggregator rails allow Stockbridge's verified voice+UPI sales logs to eventually unlock formal working capital for kiranas.
* **JanAI (janai.ai) Cafés Model:**
  * **Overview:** Operated by JanAI Technology Pvt. Ltd. (Bengaluru) with field partner Head Held High Foundation and EkStep Foundation ([JanAI, 2026](https://janai.ai/)).
  * **Footprint:** **10 JanAI Cafés** live as of late 2026 (first launched in Mysuru in July 2026), with an MoU signed in **October 2026** to launch **25 new AI Cafés across Eastern Uttar Pradesh** ([KDEM, Jul 2026](https://karnatakadigital.in/news/janai-cafe-launched-in-mysuru-to-bring-ai-learning-to-rural-communities/); [Travel2Films, Oct 2026](https://travel2films.com/bringing-ai-to-rural-india-janai-to-establish-25-new-ai-cafes-across-eastern-uttar-pradesh/)).
  * **Operating Model:** Combines a voice-first app (**JanAI Asha**, supporting 8 Indian languages on ₹8,000 handsets) with a local physical café and a trained local ambassador (**Doot**). **75%** of households complete a journey when a Doot sits with them ([JanAI, 2026](https://janai.ai/how-it-works/)).
  * **Monetisation:** Free for citizens; providers pay only on verified outcomes, with **33%–66% of revenue** shared locally with the café operator/ambassador ([JanAI, 2026](https://janai.ai/how-it-works/)). *Lesson for Stockbridge:* Voice AI alone has high drop-off; pairing voice AI with a human "Doot" (or distributor field agent) for the first 1–2 weeks drives 75%+ activation.

## 7. Bottom-Up Market Size (Punjab → India)

### Stated Assumptions **[Estimate]**
* **Pricing:** **₹150 per active shop per month** (₹1,800/shop/year) paid by the distributor. (At 200 active shops, a distributor pays ₹30,000/month—justifiable if Stockbridge recovers even ₹50,000–₹1,00,000/month in near-expiry stock reallocation and claim reduction).
* **Serviceable Active Shops per Distributor:** Average **150 active shops** per mid-to-large FMCG distributor.
* **Addressable Share (SAM):** **30%** of distributors are mid-to-large enough (₹5 Cr+ turnover) to pay for software.

## 8. Job Creation & Field Onboarding-Agent Economics

### Comparable Gig / Merchant Onboarding Rates in India
* **Paytm Service Agent (PSA):** Pays **₹125** for initial merchant onboarding (active for 1 day) + **₹175** once the merchant becomes "engaged" (10+ payments) = **₹300 total per engaged merchant**; plus **₹150–₹200** for Soundbox installation and **₹100–₹200** for merchant revisits ([FirstGuide / Paytm PSA](https://firstguide.in/paytm-service-agent-commission-chart/); [Paytm PSA](https://paytm.com/psa)).

### Proposed Stockbridge Helper / Onboarding-Agent Model **[Estimate]**
* **Payout Structure:**
  * **₹100** upon shop onboarding (app installed, voice + UPI permissions granted, first 5 voice bills logged).
  * **₹150** activation bonus if the shop logs voice-verified sales for **14+ active days** in Month 1.
  * **Total CAC per Active Shop:** **₹250** (recovered in <2 months at ₹150/month subscription).
* **Agent Monthly Earnings:** A part-time student or local gig helper onboarding **3 shops/day** (60 shops/month, with 75% activation) can earn **₹12,750–₹15,000/month** part-time—competitive with entry-level gig work in Tier-2/3 Punjab towns while creating local youth employment.
