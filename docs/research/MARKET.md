## How to read the labels
| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED** | Number found at the linked source, and the source is a news outlet, government body, trade body or research firm |
| **VERIFIED (secondary)** | Number found, but on a page quoting the original (for example, a blog quoting NPCI). Fine for the deck; cite the original if a judge asks |
| **VENDOR CLAIM** | Number found, but on a page selling software that profits from the problem. Use only with the label "industry estimate" |
| **ESTIMATE** | Our own assumption or calculation. Always say "our estimate" on a slide |
| **WRONG / NOT FOUND** | The raw report's number is not at the source, or the source says something different. Do not use |

**What distributors pay for software today** ([TechArdent Labs, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.techardentlabs.com/insights/busy-vs-marg-vs-tallyprime), excl. GST). — VERIFIED (secondary; confirm on vendor sites before quoting)

| Software | Price |
|---|---|

| Marg ERP 9+ | ₹5,550 (Nano) to ₹26,000 (Gold); ₹3,000 per extra user |

**Who already pays per kirana:** brands spend ₹200–250 per kirana per month on digital enablement, according to ONDC ([newsletter](https://ondc.org/public/assets/newsletters/ondc-fy25-26-in-focus.pdf)). — VERIFIED (ONDC's claim)

**ROI check at ₹150/active shop/month** (ESTIMATE; this is the slide judges will push on)
| Distributor | Fee per year | Expiry loss (vendor estimate) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹15 Cr turnover, 200 active shops | ₹3.6 lakh | ₹22–45 lakh | Pays back if Stockbridge cuts losses by 8–16%. But the fee equals 6–12% of the distributor's net profit (2–4% net = ₹30–60 lakh) |
| ₹2.4 Cr turnover (₹20 lakh/month), 150 shops | ₹2.7 lakh | ₹1.2–4.8 lakh | **Doesn't work.** The fee is close to the distributor's entire expiry loss |

**What this means:**
1. Distributors today pay ₹5,000–70,000 for software, mostly once. ₹3.6 lakh a year is a big jump.
2. Target distributors above about ₹10 Cr turnover first.
3. Consider pricing on outcomes, like JanAI does: a share of the value of stock actually moved.
4. Brands, who already pay ₹200–250 per kirana, may be a second payer.

Put this on the business-model slide before a judge raises it.

| Product | What it captures | Who pays | Price | Moves stock between shops? | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Bizom and other DMS** | Field orders, beats, secondary sales | Brands | Not public | No | ESTIMATE |
| **Udaan, Jumbotail, ElasticRun, JioMart Partner** | B2B wholesale ordering and credit | Margin on goods | Built into price | No (one-way supply) | ESTIMATE |
| **ONDC DigiDukaan** | Kirana procurement across brands and distributors | Free to retailers | Free | No | VERIFIED (section 7) |

**The raw report got VoiceKhata wrong.** It listed ₹199/month or ₹1,399/year. The site now says free for every shop with no bill limit. A free voice-billing app that supports Punjabi is your closest competitor on the shop side.

**Why nobody does this yet:**
- Every product in the table sits on one side of the problem.
  - Shop apps (VoiceKhata, AlertPay) know what sold, but not batches or expiry.
  - Distributor tools (Marg, ShelfLifePro) know batches and expiry, but not what each shop is selling this week.
  - B2B platforms push stock one way, down to shops.
- Moving a near-expiry carton from a slow shop to a fast one on the same route needs both pieces of data at the same time. That means item-level sales from the shop, plus batch and expiry from the distributor.
- Kiranas don't record item-level sales because typing is too slow. Voice capture, checked against the UPI amount, is what makes that data cheap enough to collect.

## 6. Quick-commerce pressure

- Market size: gross order value of **₹64,000 Cr in FY25**, up from ₹30,000 Cr in FY24 ([CareEdge](https://www.careratings.com/uploads/newsfiles/1752146049_India%E2%80%99s%20Q-commerce%20Market%20-%20CareEdge%20Advisory%20Report.pdf)). — VERIFIED
- Dark stores: **3,072** in FY25 (CareEdge) and **6,563** by Feb 2026 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/magazine/deep-dive/story/quick-commerce-firms-go-big-on-dark-stores-517751-2026-02-24)). — VERIFIED
- **67%** of 300 surveyed kirana owners report lower sales, and **$1.28 bn** moved from kiranas to quick commerce in 2024 ([Datum Intelligence](https://datumintell.com/report-quick-commerce.html)). — VERIFIED
- About **2 lakh kirana closures** in the year to Oct 2024, 45% of them in metros ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/industry/news/quick-commerce-led-to-closure-of-200-000-kirana-stores-retailers-body-124103000841_1.html)). — VERIFIED, but AICPDF has a stake in the claim, so attribute it: "AICPDF says…"
- **Where kiranas hold up** ([Indian Chamber of Commerce](https://indianchamber.org/upload/page/pdf/quick-commerce.pdf); small sample, no date). — VERIFIED
  - All respondents say they have loyal customers who rely on credit.
  - 87% cite their small-pack and sachet range as an advantage.
- **Removed:** "$12.9 B in CY2025E" (not on the cited Datum page) and the FICCI attribution for the closures (not in the FICCI PDF).
- **Angle for the pitch:** metros take most of the hit, and quick commerce is weak outside them. Non-metros contribute only about 20% of its GMV ([YourStory/Redseer](https://yourstory.com/2025/07/india-quick-commerce-booms-metros-slow-lane-non-metros)). Punjab's tier-2 and tier-3 towns are where kiranas still have time to modernise.

- **MSME rails** (Udyam, CGTMSE, Account Aggregator): verified sales logs from voice + UPI could later support credit for kiranas. A future angle, not a deck number.
- **JanAI Cafés** ([janai.ai](https://janai.ai/how-it-works/)). — VERIFIED

  - Trained local ambassadors ("Doots") sit with users. 75% of households a Doot sits with complete a journey.

  - **Lesson for Stockbridge:** pair the voice app with a human onboarder for the first weeks.

## 8. Market size (rebuilt)

**Assumptions** (all ESTIMATE):
- ₹150 per active shop per month = ₹1,800 per shop per year. Supported by brands' ₹200–250 per kirana spend.
- 30% of shops are reachable in the near term: UPI-using and on a route with a paying distributor. This is an assumption; test it in interviews.

**Headline method: count each physical shop once** (use this on the slide)
| | Shops | Reachable (30%) | Revenue per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| **India** | 1.3 crore | 39 lakh | **₹702 Cr** (~$83 M) |
| **Punjab** | ~2.85 lakh | ~85,500 | **~₹15.4 Cr** (~$1.8 M) |

**Upper bound: count every distributor that serves a shop** (appendix only)
- A kirana buys from 5–10 distributors, and each could pay for the same shop.
- 30% of distributors × 150 shops × ₹1,800:
  - India: 1.35 lakh distributors → ₹3,645 Cr;
  - Punjab: ~3,000–3,500 distributors → ₹81–95 Cr.
- It counts the same shop several times. Don't lead with it; a judge will divide it by the shop count.

**Start-up target (ESTIMATE; the team should set these numbers):**
- Patiala pilot: 1 distributor, 1 route, 10–15 shops.
- Year 1: 10 distributors × 150 shops = 1,500 shops → **₹27 lakh annual revenue**.
- Year 3: 1% of the Punjab estimate.

The raw report's "5% of the market in 3 years" (₹182 Cr) has no plan behind it. Drop it.

## 9. Jobs: onboarding agents

**Benchmark: Paytm Service Agent** ([FirstGuide, Sep 2025](https://firstguide.in/paytm-service-agent-commission-chart/)). — VERIFIED (secondary)
- ₹125 per onboarded merchant, plus ₹175 if the merchant becomes "engaged" (10 payments from 5 customers on 5 days) = **₹300**.
- Soundbox installation: ₹150–200. Revisits: ₹100–200.

**Proposed Stockbridge model** (ESTIMATE):
- ₹100 when a shop is onboarded (app installed, permissions granted, 5 voice bills logged).
- ₹150 bonus if the shop logs sales on 14+ days in month 1.
- That's a **₹250 cost to acquire each active shop**, recovered in under 2 months at ₹150/month.

**Agent earnings:** 3 shops a day × 20 days = 60 shops. At 75% activation:
- 60 × ₹100 = ₹6,000;
- 45 × ₹150 = ₹6,750;
- total **₹12,750 a month**, part-time.

The raw report's "up to ₹15,000" isn't supported by its own maths; use ₹12,750. The 75% activation rate is borrowed from JanAI's Doot figure. It's a guess for us.

## Open questions for the interviews
1. What % of purchases do you write off to expiry and damage each year? What % of claims get paid, and how long does payment take?
2. How many active shops, routes and delivery days do you have?
3. What do you pay for software today, and who decides?
4. Would ₹150 per active shop per month be a yes? Or would you rather pay a share of the stock value actually moved?
5. Kiranas: how much stock expired last month? How do you track what sold?
